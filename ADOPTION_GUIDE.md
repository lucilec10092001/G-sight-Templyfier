# Internal demonstration and adoption — Templyfier v53

Prepared plan, not a report of actual adoption or measured productivity.
Use approved internal examples; do not publish client exports or screenshots.

## A short first demonstration

1. Name the problem: repetitive topline formatting and inconsistent manual choices.
2. Show the four-step overview and upload the approved example study.
3. Open one review point. Change one label or metric, click Apply and show the
   saved choice. Explain that suggestions remain under CMI control.
4. Demonstrate one non-Color grouping and rename the shared label once. Show undo.
5. With three benchmarks, show both layouts and turn optional gaps off. Explain
   that significance remains specific to each comparison.
6. Confirm the preview, generate Excel and compare values/bases with the source.
   Open the workbook in desktop Excel to recalculate and review the final layout.
7. Explain that the first shared-server release keeps no project draft, saved
   profile or client memory. Mention server IT qualification as a separate requirement.

Avoid walking through every setting on first exposure. Give users a simple first
success, then show optional features when they solve a real project need.

## Evidence that makes the tool convincing

Measure the existing workflow and the tool on the same approved tasks: time,
manual steps, questions needing help, corrections and output differences. Do not
invent percentage savings. Ask novice and experienced users to repeat the task
without prompting. Use the CMI protocol and record concrete recurring issues.

Start with a small approved pilot. Provide the English guide and one short example
workflow, identify an internal owner/referent for reviewed team habits, and define
how issues and incompatible exports are reported. Keep production access, retention
and backup sign-off with IT. Expand after output and usability checks pass.

Reliable results, understandable choices and observed time savings are stronger
reasons for recognition than promising a perfect tool or a guaranteed award.
